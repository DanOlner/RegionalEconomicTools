Author

Dan Olner

Introduction

This doc shows job count location quotients for Sheffield, Barnsley, Doncaster and Rotherham for sectors that are part of the UK Industrial Strategy definition list (see below for more info on that).

  • For BRES, Barnsley, Doncaster and Rotherham are combined (BRES still uses ITL3 2021 zones where those three are in one zone).
  • For Companies House data (job counts extracted from accounts between June 2024 to end of June 2025), each of the four places have separate plots.

Existing (or recently ended) UKRI CCI clusters

Original projects:

  • “Business of Fashion, Textiles and Technology” has centres in London, Cambs, Chelsea, Leeds, Loughborough. (“Future Fashion Factory” is a cluster based at Leeds University Institute of Textiles and Colour.)
  • Creative Industries Policy and Evidence Centre (Newcastle University, with ‘Southern hub’): policy research+exchange across all CCI.
  • Future Screens NI: “delivering expert technical skills, opportunity and growth across film and broadcast, animation, games and immersive technologies and industries in Northern Ireland.” (Queens and University of Ulster.)
  • Creative informatics (Edinburgh): “Funding and development opportunities to creative individuals and organisations working in Edinburgh and Southeast Scotland that want to develop new products, businesses and experiences using data and data-driven technology.”
  • InGame: R&D in Dundee’s games cluster.
  • XR Stories (University of York): Extended reality research with industry, lab at York University.

More recent projects funded (Nov 2024 announcement):

  • Music Futures in Liverpool.
  • Createch Frontiers, Birmingham City, Coventry and Warwick Universities.

Funding period ended:

  • Bath/Bristol Creative R&D: screen / performance / publishing (ended 2024)
  • Clwstwr in Cardiff (ended): “Creates platforms for independent companies, SMEs, micro-businesses and freelancers to compete with global media companies.” (Some Bath/Bristol overlap)
  • StoryFutures (Surrey, Royal Holloway University, ended): “placing innovative storytelling at the heart of the next generation of immersive technologies and experiences.”

UK Industrial Strategy Linkage

The UK Industrial Strategy was released in 2025, with accompanying plans for 6 out of the 8 target “IS-8” sectors. It also has a sector definition list that provides SIC codes being targeted - top level sectors and ‘frontier’ subsectors (a few of which overlap; most do not). These range in specificity between 2 digit and 5 digit SIC codes.

As that webpage says, most of these SIC codes cannot capture the full range of possible firms the strategy aims to cover, and it also contains entire sections that SIC codes can’t cover at all - clean energy and certain digital sectors in particular. However, it does allow an analysis of which sectors are definitely within those industrial strategy categories. This section gathers job details for those specific sectors - a distinct range of types and breadth - and compares South Yorkshire to the rest of the UK where possible, to see which sectors align with the industrial strategy and other analysis above. This is done for both BRES and Companies House job counts, to pick up on different job types.

The table below lists the IS-8 broad sector headings. In the IndStrat plots, the abbreviations for these IS-8 headings appear before the sector name. Clean energy - as mentioned above - has no SIC codes and so can’t currently be matched.

History of CCI jobs and GVA in South Yorkshire

The next two plots show Creative and Cultural Industries job counts and GVA as a proportion of the total, for the four South Yorkshire places and the UK’s core cities (Great Britain’s if BRES jobs data). These show structural change, not growth directly - how each place’s economic composition has changed over time.

South Yorkshire’s four places are indicated with thicker lines.

CCI jobs as a percent of the total for core cities and South Yorkshire

Here, job counts from BRES are summed for all industrial strategy creative SIC codes to find how the percent of South Yorkshire’s CCI jobs has changed over the data’s history, alongside Great Britain core cities for comparison.

BRES has data from 2015 to 2024. A 3 year running average is used here to smooth its volatility, so the most recent data point is showing the 2022-24 average.

Some things to note here:

  • Sheffield’s CCI job count is in a lower core city group with three others, percentage-wise: Birmingham, Liverpool and Nottingham.

  • In all core cities, CCI jobs as a proportion of each city’s total jobs has been climbing, including through COVID for most, though not all.

  • Barnsley, Doncaster and Rotherham are here for comparison, but are not similar to other core cities and so not too much should be read into their position here (same for the GVA plot below).

CCI GVA as a percent of the total for core cities and South Yorkshire

Economic output for CCI sectors has to use a slightly different sector list, as the regional GVA data only has ‘58-60’ and ‘90-91’ SIC codes at ITL3 level (the level that most closely matches local authorities).

But it’s close enough to give an indication of how CCI output has changed over time. While this data goes back much further, to 1998, it is show here for the same time range as the BRES data, for direct comparison.

  • Out of the core cities, Sheffield and Tyneside (the ITL3 zone) have historically had the lowest proportion of their economies made up by CCI sectors. Sheffield looks to have bounced back from the pandemic better than a lot of other core cities.
  • The deepest impact of the pandemic is visible in 2020, with differing paths after that point. For example, Barnsley’s CCI GVA steadily increases its proportion (though note, we don’t know from this plot what role other sectors shrinking may have had on this; in the above plot, job proportions seem to have stayed steady).
  • Rotherham’s story looks similar in both these plots: declining job and GVA proportion for CCI sectors since the pandemic.

Location quotient plots

The outputs below are location quotient plots for each of South Yorkshire’s local authorities, for “creative” industrial strategy SIC codes.

Core cities are again included for comparison.

  • They are separated into industrial strategy ‘frontier’ sectors and the rest. Some are repeated, as the IndStrat definitions have two overlapping, nested categories (591 and 62011 are frontier sectors, but their parent sectors, 59 and 6201, are listed in the broader creative sectors; both are used here).

Here’s a guide to reading them:

  • Location quotients for sectoral job proportions are shown for SY local authorities
  • Sectors to the right of the centre line are more concentrated in this region when compared to the national average. (e.g. LQ = 10, it is 10x more concentrated)
  • Green circles have been increasing in concentration over time (larger circles increasing the most)
  • Red circles have been decreasing in concentration over time (larger circles decreasing the most)
  • Black horizontal lines show the min and max of concentration in the data’s history (from 2015 to 2023)
  • Circles and lines can be read together - for example, if a green circle is at the right of this line, sector job concentration has been increasing on average and is continuing.
  • Per sector job counts and percent of the total local authority job count are shown on the right.
  • Background squares are LQs for core cities. Core cities’ first two letters are used. Here’s a list of those, for reference:
