# Universities, the Industrial Strategy and devolution: the Yorkshire & Humber knot

*Compiled June 2026. A companion to [mittelstand.md](mittelstand.md) and [uk_industrial_strategy_since_launch.md](uk_industrial_strategy_since_launch.md). This note works one specific seam: how universities connect to the Industrial Strategy and the devolution agenda — through skills, research commercialisation and place — and why that connection is strained by the worst university financial crisis in a generation, with the Yorkshire & Humber picture (and Peter O'Brien's Yorkshire Universities) as the worked example.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The paradox in one paragraph

The Modern Industrial Strategy leans on universities harder than any previous UK industrial policy — to **commercialise research, supply high-level skills, and anchor place-based growth**. Read through the Mittelstand lens of the companion notes, universities (especially their applied/Catapult tier) are the closest thing the UK has to the German **Fraunhofer + Fachhochschule** layer that the Mittelstand depends on. Yet they are being asked to do *more* at exactly the moment a centrally-set funding crisis is forcing them to do *less* — and the cuts fall hardest on the regional and post-92 institutions that a non-London-centric strategy needs most. Devolution can **redeploy** this capacity; it cannot, as currently configured, **refinance** it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. How universities plug into the Industrial Strategy

The IS gives universities three explicit jobs ([Advance HE's "Governor View"](https://www.advance-he.ac.uk/knowledge-hub/governor-view-uks-modern-industrial-strategy) summarises them well): **commercialising research** into new technologies; providing the **pipeline of high-level skills** for the IS-8 sectors; and acting as **engines for place-based growth**.

The money and the levers behind this:

-   **£86bn of R&D** to 2029/30 (from the Spending Review), targeted at the IS-8 and meant to crowd in private investment — see [CaSE's analysis of R&D in the strategy](https://www.sciencecampaign.org.uk/analysis-and-publications/detail/rd-in-the-2025-industrial-strategy/).
-   **UKRI and Innovate UK are increasingly *requiring* alignment** — universities' research, innovation and skills strategies are expected to map onto the [sector plans](https://www.port.ac.uk/news-events-and-blogs/blogs/research-culture/shaping-the-future-how-uk-industrial-strategy-sector-plans-will-transform-research-and-innovation) to access funding and partnerships.
-   **Higher Education Innovation Funding (HEIF)** — \~£280m in 2024/25 — is the core knowledge-exchange stream. The returns are large: roughly **£14.80 per £1** invested (up to \~£20 at large research-intensive universities), per the [IfM knowledge-exchange evidence](https://www.ifm.eng.cam.ac.uk/research/uci-policy-unit/knowledgehub/) and [Russell Group](https://www.russellgroup.ac.uk/policy/policy-briefings/driving-growth-through-regional-innovation-clusters).
-   **Spinouts and clusters** — Russell Group spinouts raised **£1.66bn of equity in 2023** (\~9.5% of all UK equity), per the [Russell Group spinouts briefing](https://www.russellgroup.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-02/RGbriefing_Spinouts_July24.pdf); incubators like SETsquared show the cluster-building model.
-   **The £500m [Local Innovation Partnerships Fund](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/regional-leaders-to-get-more-power-over-innovation-funding-to-drive-local-jobs-and-growth)** explicitly routes innovation money toward "high-potential clusters" and the devolved tier — the place where universities, mayors and firms are meant to meet.

The sector welcomed the framing — see the [Russell Group response](https://www.russellgroup.ac.uk/news/response-uks-modern-industrial-strategy) and the [NCUB 2025 policy digest](https://www.ncub.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/6642_NCUB_Policy_Digests_Research_Innovation_V3.pdf) — but the recurring caveat is that alignment-conditional funding concentrates resource on the IS-8 and the most research-intensive institutions, leaving the rest exposed (a theme that returns in §4).

## 2. The skills pipeline — where the Mittelstand comparison bites hardest

The companion note ([mittelstand.md](mittelstand.md), §4) flags the **dual vocational training system as "the single most load-bearing institution in the German story"** and the clearest place the UK falls short; its [§6](mittelstand.md) now sets out that pipeline end-to-end — the dual apprenticeship, the chambers, BIBB-set occupational standards, the *Meister*→*Fachhochschule*→Fraunhofer ladder, and the co-financing economics. This section works the *university* end of the same institution. The point sharpens here because in Germany skills, applied research and the Mittelstand are bound together by a **thick middle tier** the UK doesn't really have:

-   **Dual vocational training (apprenticeships)** — firm + vocational school, an international benchmark — feeds technicians directly into Mittelstand firms.
-   **Universities of applied sciences (*Fachhochschulen* / HAW)** — regionally rooted, applied, teaching-and-transfer institutions — supply applied engineers and run [dual-study programmes](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/universities/dual-studies/) combining university study with workplace practice.
-   **Fraunhofer institutes** sit alongside them, doing applied R&D *for* the Mittelstand and running their own [dual studies](https://www.fraunhofer.de/en/jobs-and-career/students_trainees/dualstudies.html) and [collaborations with the applied-science universities](https://www.fraunhofer.de/en/about-fraunhofer/profile-structure/cooperation-with-universities.html).

The UK system is, by contrast, **bimodal**: research-intensive, degree-focused universities at one end; a chronically underfunded, churned FE/apprenticeship system at the other; and a thin, contested space in between. The "missing middle" in firms (the Mittelstand diagnosis) is mirrored by a **missing middle in skills institutions**.

The IS-era reforms are trying to thicken that middle — **Skills England** (the new national body), the move from the apprenticeship levy to a **Growth and Skills Levy**, the **devolved Adult Skills Fund**, **Local Skills Improvement Plans (LSIPs)**, and university **degree apprenticeships** — but these are recent, under-resourced, and not yet a coherent dual system. Universities sit awkwardly across the divide: they *do* deliver high-level and degree-apprenticeship skills, yet the financial crisis (§4) is pushing them to cut exactly the expensive STEM and technical provision the IS-8 most needs.

## 3. Universities as the UK's Fraunhofer/Fachhochschule analogue — the Catapult and AMRC layer

If you ask "what is the UK's nearest equivalent to a Fraunhofer institute serving regional industry?", the honest answer is the **Catapult network** — and the standout, Yorkshire-based example is the [**University of Sheffield Advanced Manufacturing Research Centre (AMRC)**](https://www.amrc.co.uk/).

-   The AMRC is part of the [**High Value Manufacturing Catapult**](https://hvm.catapult.org.uk/centre/advanced-manufacturing-research-centre-amrc/), founded in 2001, explicitly on a **Fraunhofer-inspired collaborative-research model**, working with "manufacturing companies of any size."
-   It employs **500+ engineers/researchers**, contributes [\~£55m a year and works with 120+ industrial partners](https://sheffield.ac.uk/news/university-sheffields-role-delivering-modern-industrial-strategy) — Boeing, Rolls-Royce, McLaren, BAE, Airbus — and turned a derelict Rotherham steel site into the **Advanced Manufacturing Park**, now anchoring the [**South Yorkshire Investment Zone**](https://sheffield.ac.uk/news/university-sheffield-amrc-announces-largest-ever-research-project-part-new-south-yorkshire).
-   It delivers exactly the things the Mittelstand model relies on: **applied, incremental process innovation** (cutting a component from 40 hours to 3), **skills** (the AMRC Training Centre, semiconductor courses), and **translational links** that help firms — not just spinouts — adopt technology.

This is the single best UK demonstration that the "missing applied-research layer" *can* be built, and that it maps directly onto an IS-8 cluster (advanced manufacturing/defence) in a Northern Growth Strategy priority place. **But the contrast with Fraunhofer is instructive**: the Catapults are **nationally funded** (Innovate UK core grant), few in number, and not regionally governed; Fraunhofer is far larger, denser, and reaches into small towns (the distributed geography of [mittelstand.md §4](mittelstand.md) and [§8 of the IS note](uk_industrial_strategy_since_launch.md)). The UK has proof-of-concept, not coverage.

## 4. The financial crisis — being asked to do more with less

The timing is brutal. As the IS makes universities load-bearing for growth, the sector is in its sharpest financial crisis in a generation:

-   The **Office for Students** projects **\~45% of providers (\~124 institutions) in deficit in 2025-26**, with nearly one in six holding under 30 days' liquidity, and \~50 providers at risk within 2–3 years — see [Universities UK](https://www.universitiesuk.ac.uk/what-we-do/creating-voice-our-members/media-releases/universities-grip-financial-crisis-what) and the Education Committee's [insolvency inquiry](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5901/cmselect/cmeduc/807/report.html).
-   **12,000+ jobs were cut in 2025**, with course, module and whole-department closures across the sector ([PIE News](https://thepienews.com/job-cuts-mount-across-uk-universities-amid-financial-strain/)).
-   The causes are **national policy levers**: home fees frozen since 2017 (≈ −25% in real terms), a post-2023 fall in international students, and — counterproductively — the **October 2025 international student levy**, which raises money from the very recruitment universities depend on, worsening deficits ([THE on the crisis](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/depth/will-funding-crisis-confine-uk-research-elite-universities)).

Two consequences matter for the strategy:

1.  **The "do more, with less" paradox.** Commercialisation, civic engagement and expensive STEM/technical teaching are precisely the IS-relevant activities most exposed when a university retrenches to its financial core.
2.  **A geographic bite (cf. [§8 of the IS note](uk_industrial_strategy_since_launch.md)).** The crisis falls hardest on **regional and post-92 universities** — the distributed institutional layer that a non-monocentric, town-reaching strategy needs most. A national funding model is therefore **hollowing out the very dispersed capacity** the Northern Growth Strategy implicitly relies on, concentrating research resilience in a handful of elite (largely southern) institutions.

## 5. The Yorkshire & Humber picture — strongest model, deepest distress

Yorkshire & Humber is where this tension is most visible, because the region has simultaneously built the **best UK model of university–region integration** and is suffering some of the **deepest financial distress**.

**The integration model.** [Yorkshire Universities](https://yorkshireuniversities.ac.uk/) — the 12-HEI membership body led by **Dr Peter O'Brien** (the author of the Northern Growth Strategy analysis cited in the [companion IS note](uk_industrial_strategy_since_launch.md)) — has positioned universities as **anchor institutions and capacity-providers** for the new devolved system ([on the English Devolution White Paper](https://yorkshireuniversities.ac.uk/2025/01/20/the-english-devolution-white-paper/); [Universities Scotland reflections](https://www.universities-scotland.ac.uk/yorkshire-universities-reflections/)). The flagship vehicle is [**Y-PERN, the Yorkshire & Humber Policy Engagement and Research Network**](https://y-pern.org.uk/category/yorkshire-humber/) — embedding university researchers directly inside combined and local authorities to supply the analytical capacity thin MSAs lack, aligning local-authority strategies with combined-authority Local Growth Plans. Allied initiatives include YPIP, the Yorkshire Policy Commission, the [York Policy Engine](https://yorkshireuniversities.ac.uk/2026/02/23/the-york-policy-engine-regional-growth-collaboration-and-community/), the **White Rose** Leeds–Sheffield–York partnership, and the **AMRC** (§3). Wonkhe's framing — that [universities collaborating for their regions is becoming a policy imperative](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/universities-collaborating-for-the-good-of-our-regions-is-fast-becoming-a-policy-imperative/) — describes Yorkshire better than almost anywhere.

**The distress.** Yet Yorkshire & Humber HE faces a **combined deficit of £150m+** and heavy cuts ([UNISON regional spotlight](https://yorks.unison.org.uk/2025/04/11/spotlight-higher-education-in-yorkshire-and-humberside/); [TheBusinessDesk](https://www.thebusinessdesk.com/yorkshire/news/2137562-union-warns-of-hundreds-of-redundancies-at-yorkshire-universities)):

-   **Bradford** — \~£16m deficit, compulsory redundancies, \~200–300 posts (\~20% of staff), course closures ([UNISON](https://yorks.unison.org.uk/2025/04/04/job-cut-proposals-at-university-of-bradford-would-leave-institution-unrecognisable-warns-unison/)).
-   **Hull** — \~£23m deficit, \~206 departures.
-   **Sheffield** — \~1,300 staff affected by restructuring; **Sheffield Hallam** — 500+ posts gone, strike action over a \~£27m saving.
-   **York** — \~270 via voluntary severance (£34m savings target); **Leeds Beckett** \~201; **Huddersfield** \~180.

So the region asked to host AMRC-grade applied research, supply IS-8 skills, and staff the devolved policy machine is, at the same time, shedding the staff who would do it. **The institutions are the substrate and the substrate is shrinking.**

## 6. How devolution, universities and strategy could develop together

Pulling the three notes together, there are five moves — roughly in ascending order of difficulty — that would let these develop as a system rather than at cross-purposes:

1.  **Institutionalise the Y-PERN capacity model.** Make university-supplied analytical and delivery capacity a standing feature of MSAs (business boards, LSIPs, Local Growth Plans), not a series of grant-funded pilots. This directly answers the **capacity-and-consolidation** gap in [mittelstand.md §5](mittelstand.md) — the German system works partly because regional institutions have durable capacity; embedded universities are England's cheapest route to some of it.
2.  **Treat the Catapult/AMRC tier as the UK's Fraunhofer analogue and resource it regionally.** Expand applied-research provision aimed at the **mid-market and the supply chain** (not just spinouts and primes), so it reaches the [hidden-champion and succession-risk firms](mittelstand.md) the companion note identifies — the part of the Mittelstand model the UK has *proven* but not *scaled*.
3.  **Build a regional "dual system" out of existing parts.** Knit the **devolved Adult Skills Fund + Growth and Skills Levy + LSIPs + degree apprenticeships + FE + university provision** into something closer to the German dual model, governed at MSA level with employers — the load-bearing institution [mittelstand.md §4](mittelstand.md) says is missing.
4.  **Use firm-level data to target the linkages.** Wire the [Companies House mid-market evidence base (mittelstand.md §7)](mittelstand.md) into Y-PERN and university KE teams, so KTPs, spinouts and AMRC-style engagements are matched to **actual regional firms** rather than allocated against broad sector averages.
5.  **Face the funding-model constraint honestly.** The binding levers — home fees, the international levy, QR/UKRI allocations, Catapult core grants — are **national**. Devolution can *redeploy* university capacity into regional growth; it cannot *refinance* the universities themselves. This is the **fiscal/constitutional gap of [mittelstand.md §5](mittelstand.md) reappearing in the HE domain**: a genuinely *Land*-like settlement would put a multi-year regional applied-research-and-skills budget on a stable footing, surviving electoral and admissions cycles. Nothing currently on the table does that.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. The reading in one line

The Industrial Strategy has made universities load-bearing for regional growth — research commercialisation, high-level skills, anchor institutions — and they are the nearest UK equivalent to the Fraunhofer/Fachhochschule layer the Mittelstand depends on; but a centrally-set funding crisis is dismantling that layer, hardest in the very regions (Yorkshire & Humber among them) that the Northern Growth Strategy needs it most, and devolution as configured can redeploy this capacity without being able to refinance it.
